# The Period 

It was the best of times, it was the worst of times, it was the age of wisdom, it was the age of foolishness, it was the epoch of belief, it was the epoch of incredulity, it was the season of Light, it was the season of Darkness, it was the spring of hope, it was the winter of despair, we had everything before us, we had nothing before us, we were all going direct to Heaven, we were all going direct the other way--in short, the period was so far like the present period, that some of its noisiest authorities insisted on its being received, for good or for evil, in the superlative _(superlative /suːˈpɜːlətɪv/: the highest or most extreme degree)_ degree of comparison only. 

There were a king with a large jaw and a queen with a plain face, on the throne of England; there were a king with a large jaw and a queen with a fair face, on the throne of France. In both countries it was clearer than crystal to the lords of the State preserves of loaves _(loaf /ləʊf/: pieces of baked bread)_ and fishes, that things in general were settled for ever. 

It was the year of Our Lord one thousand seven hundred and seventy-five. Spiritual revelations _(revelation /ˌrɛvəˈleɪʃən/: surprising or hidden truths that are made known)_ were conceded _(concede /kənˈsiːd/: admitted or accepted as true)_ to England at that favoured period, as at this. Mrs. Southcott had recently attained her five-and-twentieth blessed birthday, of whom a prophetic _(prophetic /prəˈfɛtɪk/: claiming to predict the future)_ private in the Life Guards had heralded _(herald /ˈhɛrəld/: announced or signalled something important)_ the sublime _(sublime /səˈblaɪm/: extremely impressive or grand)_ appearance by announcing that arrangements were made for the swallowing up of London and Westminster. Even the Cock-lane ghost had been laid only a round dozen of years, after rapping out _(rap /rap/: hitting or knocking sharply to make sounds)_ its messages, as the spirits of this very year last past (supernaturally deficient in originality) rapped out theirs. Mere messages in the earthly order of events had lately come to the English Crown and People, from a congress of British subjects in America: which, strange to relate, have proved more important to the human race than any communications yet received through any of the chickens of the Cock-lane brood. 

France, less favoured on the whole as to matters spiritual than her sister of the shield and trident _(trident /ˈtraɪdənt/: a three-pointed spear (symbol of naval power))_, rolled with exceeding smoothness down hill, making paper money and spending it. Under the guidance of her Christian pastors _(pastor /ˈpɑːstə/: religious leaders or ministers)_, she entertained herself, besides, with such humane achievements as sentencing a youth to have his hands cut off, his tongue torn out with pincers _(pincer /ˈpɪnsə/: tools used to grip or pull tightly)_, and his body burned alive, because he had not kneeled down in the rain to do honour to a dirty procession of monks which passed within his view, at a distance of some fifty or sixty yards. It is likely enough that, rooted in the woods of France and Norway, there were growing trees, when that sufferer was put to death, already marked by the Woodman, Fate, to come down and be sawn into boards, to make a certain movable framework with a sack and a knife in it, terrible in history. It is likely enough that in the rough outhouses of some tillers _(tiller /ˈtɪlə/: people who farm or work the land)_ of the heavy lands adjacent to Paris, there were sheltered from the weather that very day, rude carts _(cart /kɑːt/: simple vehicles used for carrying goods)_, bespattered _(bespatter /bɪˈspatə/: covered with splashes of mud or dirt)_ with rustic _(rustic /ˈrʌstɪk/: relating to the countryside or farming life)_ mire _(mire /ˈmaɪə/: deep wet mud)_, snuffed _(snuff /snʌf/: sniffed or smelled noisily)_ about by pigs, and roosted _(roost /ruːst/: settled or rested (of birds))_ in by poultry _(poultry /ˈpəʊltrɪ/: farm birds such as chickens)_, which the Farmer, Death, had already set apart to be his tumbrils _(tumbril /ˈtʌmbrɪl/: carts used to carry prisoners to execution)_ of the Revolution. But that Woodman and that Farmer, though they work unceasingly _(unceasingly /ʌnˈsiːsɪŋlɪ/: without stopping)_, work silently, and no one heard them as they went about with muffled _(muffle /ˈmʌfəl/: made quieter or softer)_ tread: the rather, forasmuch as to entertain any suspicion that they were awake, was to be atheistical _(atheistical /ˌeɪθɪˈɪstɪkəl/: denying belief in God)_ and traitorous _(traitorous /ˈtreɪtərəs/: showing betrayal of one’s country)_. 

In England, there was scarcely an amount of order and protection to justify much national boasting. Daring burglaries _(burglary /ˈbɜːɡlərɪ/: crimes of breaking into buildings to steal)_ by armed men, and highway robberies, took place in the capital itself every night; families were publicly cautioned not to go out of town without removing their furniture to upholsterers _(upholsterer /ʌpˈhəʊlstərə/: people who cover furniture with fabric)_’ warehouses for security; the highwayman in the dark was a City tradesman in the light, and, being recognised and challenged by his fellow-tradesman whom he stopped in his character of “the Captain,” gallantly _(gallantly /ˈɡaləntlɪ/: bravely or boldly)_ shot him through the head and rode away; the mail was waylaid _(waylay /ˈweɪleɪ/: attacked unexpectedly on the road)_ by seven robbers, and the guard shot three dead, and then got shot dead himself by the other four, “in consequence of the failure of his ammunition _(ammunition /ˌamjʊˈnɪʃən/: bullets or weapons used in fighting)_:” after which the mail was robbed in peace; that magnificent potentate _(potentate /ˈpəʊtənteɪt/: a powerful ruler)_, the Lord Mayor of London, was made to stand and deliver on Turnham Green, by one highwayman, who despoiled _(despoil /dɪˈspɔɪl/: robbed or stripped of possessions)_ the illustrious creature in sight of all his retinue _(retinue /ˈrɛtɪnjuː/: a group of followers or attendants)_; prisoners in London gaols _(gaol /dʒeɪl/: old spelling of jail or prison)_ fought battles with their turnkeys _(turnkey /ˈtɜːnkɪ/: prison guards)_, and the majesty of the law fired blunderbusses _(blunderbuss /ˈblʌndəbʌs/: old short guns with wide barrels)_ in among them, loaded with rounds of shot and ball; thieves snipped _(snip /snɪp/: cut quickly with scissors)_ off diamond crosses from the necks of noble lords at Court drawing-rooms; musketeers _(musketeer /ˌmʌskɪˈtɪə/: soldiers armed with muskets)_ went into St. Giles’s, to search for contraband _(contraband /ˈkɒntrəbænd/: illegal or smuggled goods)_ goods, and the mob _(mob /mɒb/: a large angry crowd)_ fired on the musketeers, and the musketeers fired on the mob, and nobody thought any of these occurrences much out of the common way. In the midst of them, the hangman, ever busy and ever worse than useless, was in constant requisition _(requisition /ˌrɛkwɪˈzɪʃən/: official demand or request)_; now, stringing up long rows of miscellaneous _(miscellaneous /ˌmɪsəˈleɪnɪəs/: of many different kinds)_ criminals; now, hanging a housebreaker on Saturday who had been taken on Tuesday; now, burning people in the hand at Newgate by the dozen, and now burning pamphlets _(pamphlet /ˈpamflɪt/: small printed booklets)_ at the door of Westminster Hall; to-day, taking the life of an atrocious _(atrocious /əˈtrəʊʃəs/: extremely cruel or terrible)_ murderer, and to-morrow of a wretched _(wretched /ˈrɛtʃɪd/: very unhappy or miserable)_ pilferer _(pilferer /ˈpɪlfərə/: someone who steals small things)_ who had robbed a farmer’s boy of sixpence. 

All these things, and a thousand like them, came to pass in and close upon the dear old year one thousand seven hundred and seventy-five. Environed by them, while the Woodman and the Farmer worked unheeded _(unheeded /ʌnˈhiːdɪd/: ignored or not noticed)_, those two of the large jaws, and those other two of the plain and the fair faces, trod _(tread /trɒd/: walked on)_ with stir enough, and carried their divine rights with a high hand. Thus did the year one thousand seven hundred and seventy-five conduct their Greatnesses, and myriads _(myriad /ˈmɪrɪəd/: very large numbers)_ of small creatures--the creatures of this chronicle among the rest--along the roads that lay before them.
